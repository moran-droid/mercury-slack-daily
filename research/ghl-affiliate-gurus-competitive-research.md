All three run the **same machine** with different skins:

> Free education (YouTube/course/snapshots) → co-branded GoHighLevel extended-trial page → bonus stack gated on signing up under their affiliate link → **40% recurring commission for the life of every account** → Skool community as churn insurance and tier-2 (5%) sub-affiliate recruiting → paid upsells (snapshots, memberships, courses) for everyone else.

**The economics underneath (verified):** HighLevel pays **40% monthly recurring** (=$38.80 / $118.80 / $198.80 per month on the $97/$297/$497 plans) plus **5% tier-2**, 90-day cookie, last-click, paid on the 15th. HighLevel has paid **$115M+ to affiliates since 2018, $6M in August 2025 alone**. 1,000 active referrals at a realistic plan mix ≈ **~$95K/mo**; 100 ≈ ~$9.5K/mo. Churn is the killer — hype-driven signups mass-churn inside 60 days, which is why every serious operator wraps a community around the link. Third-party benchmark: 200–500 active referrals → $25–60K/mo.

**Two strategic reads:**
1. **The commissions are the annuity; the services are the profit.** Keaton Walker (a top affiliate who literally became HighLevel's Director of BD) says outright the winners sell setup/coaching *around* the link. One widely-cited case: $150K from 3 YouTube videos, majority NOT from commissions.
2. **The affiliate program is HighLevel's actual growth engine** — CEO Shaun Clark: "coaches recommending us to their students and agency owners recommending us to other agency owners… that has literally been our growth plan." The gurus are the company's outsourced sales force, and HighLevel amplifies its best ones with co-branded 30-day-trial pages, blog features, and summit stages — that co-marketing is the real moat to compete for.

**What's replicable for Acquisitions.com:** the mechanics are commodity (bonus stack, extended trial, Skool). The saturated angle is "start an SMMA/AI agency." Nobody in the top-affiliate roster owns the **"buy a business / M&A / roll-up"** angle — your existing audience (business buyers who need CRM + follow-up + reactivation for the companies they acquire) is a differentiated, higher-LTV cohort than the make-money-online crowd these guys churn through.

## Implications for Acquisitions.com

1. **The playbook is fully documented and commodity** — free course + snapshot stack + extended-trial page + Skool. The scarce assets are (a) a differentiated audience/angle and (b) HighLevel co-marketing status (custom gohighlevel.com pages, Affiliate Insider, LevelUp stage). Those are earned with referral volume — Dom got his first co-branded page after ~100 referrals in a week.
2. **Nobody owns the M&A angle.** Every top affiliate sells "start an agency/SaaS from zero" to beginners — the highest-churn cohort in the ecosystem. Business *buyers* (Moran's audience) already own revenue-generating companies that need CRM, reactivation, reviews, and follow-up — i.e., agency-quality referrals with 2%-not-5% monthly churn and $297+ plan fit. "The CRM stack for the business you just bought" (+ DFY snapshot for HVAC/home services, which Moran already targets) is an open lane.
3. **The money is in the wrap, not the link.** Every serious operator monetizes 2–4 ways: commissions + community + snapshots + DFY services. Clay's model (paid community that's free via the link, $800 lifetime buyout for holdouts) is the cleanest architecture to copy.
4. **Watch the constraint:** no brand-bidding PPC, last-click 90-day cookie (bonus stacks exist to win the *last* click), and commissions exclude usage revenue — SaaS-mode rebilling upside goes to the agency owner, not the affiliate.
